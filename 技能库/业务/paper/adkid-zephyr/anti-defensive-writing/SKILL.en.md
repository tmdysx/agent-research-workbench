# Anti-defensive writing: contributions and evidence

Use the text and evidence supplied by the author to state supported contributions clearly and limit local observations to the conditions actually studied. Adapt the upstream narrative-focus method by removing emotional self-undermining and unsupported verdicts about the entire work while preserving scientific meaning.

## When to use

- Polish or shorten manuscript passages; inspect the narrative in an abstract, introduction, results, discussion or conclusion.
- Organize existing experimental results or draft a reviewer response from existing evidence.

This skill handles expression and structure. It does not choose research questions, change experimental plans or decide submission. Use only manuscripts and evidence authorized for the current task; do not scan unrelated materials.

## Default approach

1. Establish the passage, editing scope and available evidence. Preserve the author's wording habits, paragraph structure and technical terminology by default; make only the necessary minimal edits.
2. Separate emotional self-undermining, evidenced facts or comparisons, and necessary limits on interpretation or applicability. Remove the first when appropriate; retain the latter two accurately.
3. Organize the logic as “why the problem matters → established gap → contribution → evidence and conditions.” State advantages only within the supported scope; every paragraph need not claim a win.
4. Return the edited passage and a brief explanation of material changes. Flag missing evidence or offer conditional wording instead of turning a guess into a result. If substantial restructuring is needed, first describe concrete changes; the current task's authorized scope determines whether to apply them.

## Editing decisions

- Remove emotional phrases such as “unfortunately” or “we must admit.” Retain “only” when it conveys sample size, conditions, quantities or statistical meaning.
- Replace an unsupported inference from a local shortfall to a verdict on the whole method with a conditional comparison. Do not turn lower accuracy, failure to exceed a baseline or a nonsignificant difference into an advantage.
- Preserve numbers, units, denominators, datasets, comparators, statistical conclusions, confidence intervals and what each citation supports. Rewording must not expand experimental coverage.
- Retain “not evaluated,” “under these conditions” and “the evidence does not support” when they affect generalization, validity, interpretation or safety. This also applies to abstracts and conclusions.
- Explain a trade-off only with actual evidence, such as cost and performance measured under comparable conditions. An untested explanation for an advantage remains a candidate explanation; do not invent mechanisms, causal relationships or ablation results.
- Narrow claims or shift narrative emphasis when justified. Do not replace prespecified metrics, necessary baselines or comparison conditions because the results are unfavorable; label new post hoc exploration and explain the selection.
- An experiment may support confirmation, falsification, mechanism tests, alternative explanations, robustness, failure boundaries or reproducibility. Report necessary negative results. Placement in the main text or supplement may change within the task, but this does not authorize deleting raw results or experimental records.
- State the contribution and its evidence boundaries in the conclusion. Avoid fresh unsupported verdicts against the whole work and retain limitations that affect the central conclusion.

## Before returning the edit

Compare with the input: are numbers and comparisons unchanged; do uncertainty and necessary limitations remain; was an observation turned into causation; was an advantage invented; were important negative results or necessary baselines removed; did the edit exceed the authorized scope? Report only checks actually performed. File-format validation is not scientific or model-effectiveness validation.

Read the [bilingual editing examples](references/examples.md) when these distinctions are unclear. The three cases are fictional editing examples, not model runs or research findings.
