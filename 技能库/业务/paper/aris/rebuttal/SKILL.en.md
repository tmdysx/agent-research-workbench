# Reviewer Response and Revision Map

This is a local adaptation of pinned upstream methods. Follow the current goal, approved plan, and file scope; put actual materials/results in the author-selected workspace, not built-in folders.

## Inputs

Submitted version, complete original reviews, reply rules/limits, and confirmed new evidence.

## Workflow

1. Preserve original reviews/rules and split concerns by reviewer into clarification, evidence request, misunderstanding, limitation, or format issues.
2. Map concerns to manuscript locations, evidence, unverified points, and author choices; preserve reviewer coverage even when grouping shared issues.
3. Plan responses acknowledging real limitations, clarifying with existing evidence, or using author-approved actions; planned experiments are not completed results.
4. Draft within limits and check facts, numbers, references, and promises; self-contained context is needed for separately rendered reviewer threads.
5. Check concern coverage/length and seek author confirmation; retain prior versions/new information for follow-ups and mark unavailable independent stress tests.

## Outputs

review-issues.csv, rebuttal-strategy.md, rebuttal.md, and coverage-check.md; manuscript revision uses a separate authorized task.

## Acceptance

- Every original concern has a response with visible unresolved/unrun items.
- This round meets official character/word limits without unapproved promises.

## Continue When

Hand off to the author/authorized submitter; prepared does not mean submitted and replies are not sent automatically.

## Tools and Status

## Review coverage and actual revision

1. Preserve each reviewer comment and atomize concerns; merged responses still map back to every reviewer.
2. Bind replies to submitted/current locations, evidence, actual changes and merely planned actions under the word limit.
3. When text/bibliography or experiments are constrained, draft only supportable responses; do not claim unrun experiments or edit frozen bib entries.
