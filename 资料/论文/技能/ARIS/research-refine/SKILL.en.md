## Inputs

The user’s original problem and fixed constraints, non-goals, related methods/failure evidence, resources and acceptance criteria.

## Method

1. Freeze the problem anchor: bottleneck, non-goals, resources and success evidence. Label suggestions that change it as direction drift instead of silently replacing it.
2. Read relevant mechanisms, training setups and failure modes to identify the failing pipeline step, why naive fixes are insufficient and the smallest adequate intervention.
3. When useful, compare a minimal route with a frontier route that has a real role. Choose a dominant contribution and complexity budget; if both are weak, rethink rather than automatically combine them.
4. Make the method implementable: modules/data flow, representations, supervision/losses, training stages, inference path, component roles, failure handling and reused parts.
5. Design minimal anchor experiments, deletion/simplicity controls and refutation criteria for the mechanism. Validation serves the method rather than replacing its technical details with an experiment menu.
6. Revise against actual review feedback and preserve versions, unresolved risks and stopping points. Limit the current revision task; mark absent external review as pending instead of looping toward a high score.

## Outputs and acceptance

A problem anchor, concrete proposal, mechanism–evidence map, rejected complexity and revision record. Another reader must be able to restate the training/inference steps, without mistaking unrun ideas for findings.
